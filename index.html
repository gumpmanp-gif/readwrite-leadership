Read & Write: A Student Experience

Experience grade-level tasks first as a learner, then use Read&Write tools to reduce barriers while keeping the instructional expectation intact.

Participant Overview

This hands-on participant guide allows district leaders to experience Read&Write through the lens of a student engaging with grade-level instruction. Using activities aligned to Georgia standards in English Language Arts, Social Studies, and Biology, participants explore how specific Read&Write tools can reduce barriers to reading, vocabulary development, organization, and written expression.

Participants move through authentic instructional tasks using Text-to-Speech, Dictionary and Picture Dictionary, Highlighters and Collect Highlights, Vocabulary List, Talk&Type, Rewordify, and Simplify. Each activity demonstrates how technology can provide a different pathway to accessing and demonstrating learning while maintaining the same grade-level standard and instructional expectation.

Activity 1 • Access

Grade 7 ELA — Access the Text

First: Read the passage without using Read&Write. Notice where decoding, vocabulary, or processing begins to compete with comprehension.
As cities expand, pavement and rooftops replace soil and vegetation. These hard surfaces absorb heat and prevent rainwater from soaking naturally into the ground. During intense storms, runoff can overwhelm drainage systems and carry pollutants into streams. Some communities respond by installing green roofs—layers of plants grown on specially designed rooftops. The vegetation can moderate rooftop temperatures, retain a portion of rainfall, and provide habitat. Green roofs require appropriate building support, maintenance, and an initial investment. Even so, planners may consider them one strategy for making densely developed areas more resilient to heat and heavy rainfall.

Activity 2 • Organize Thinking

Grade 7 ELA — Compare & Contrast

Text A • Start Later

Supporters of later middle-school start times argue that adolescents often have biological sleep patterns that make early sleep difficult. More morning sleep may improve alertness and readiness to learn. However, changing the schedule may affect transportation, athletics, and family routines.

Text B • Keep Current Schedule

Supporters of the current schedule point to practical concerns. Bus routes are coordinated across grade levels, and changing one school's start time can affect transportation. Later dismissal may also reduce time for sports, jobs, or family responsibilities.

Use Highlighters + Collect Highlights. Color 1 = shared issue • Color 2 = differences. Then move the collected evidence into the organizer below.

How do the authors’ positions differ? Use evidence from both texts.

Leadership Lens: The technology can gather and organize evidence. The learner still has to analyze, compare, and explain.
Activity 3 • Vocabulary to Expression

Grade 5 Social Studies — SS5CG1 / SS5CG1.a

Citizens in a representative democracy have both rights and responsibilities. Voting is one way citizens can participate in the political process, but civic participation also includes learning about issues and communicating with elected leaders. Citizens may be called for jury duty, where they help the justice system provide a fair process. They are also expected to obey laws and contribute to their communities. These responsibilities help a democracy function because government depends on informed and involved citizens.
Highlight and build a Vocabulary List: citizen • responsibility • participate • political process • jury duty • democracy

Using at least three words from your vocabulary list, explain one responsibility of a U.S. citizen and why it matters.

Leadership Lens: What is the content standard assessing — keyboarding and spelling, or understanding of citizenship?
Activity 4 • Complex Academic Language

High School Biology — SB1.b

First: Read the original passage. Then use Rewordify or Simplify and Text-to-Speech. Compare the experience.
Before a eukaryotic cell divides, its DNA is replicated so that each chromosome consists of two identical sister chromatids. During mitosis, the duplicated chromosomes are systematically separated into two nuclei. Cytokinesis then partitions the cytoplasm, producing two daughter cells. Because each daughter cell receives a corresponding set of chromosomes, mitosis helps maintain genetic continuity during growth and tissue repair.
